Why "Nianza"

From crianza — the Spanish word for the raising of a child.

Parenthood doesn't come with a manual. We built the closest thing to one — and made sure it feels like a person, not a product. Patricia speaks English and Spanish, each in her own voice, never a translation.

The problem with the other apps

Most apps hand you either a task list or a dashboard.

Neither one is what you actually need at 2am. You don't need another thing to complete or another chart to interpret. You need someone who's done this before to talk to.

The activity app
Gives you a checklist
Today's tasks, today's activities, a progress bar. Useful for the first year. Runs thin the moment your child gets more interesting than a checklist can capture.
The tracking app
Gives you a dashboard
Every feed, every nap, every ounce — logged, charted, predicted. It tells you what your data means. It never once asks how you're doing.
Nianza
Gives you Patricia
Somewhere to log what matters, yes — but built around a person you talk to, who remembers your child, and who never once makes you feel behind.

Who she is

She's not an assistant. She's a grandmother.

Patricia has sat with new mothers at 3am, and with fathers who didn't know what they were doing, and with parents who were certain they were getting it all wrong. She has seen enough to know that most of what terrifies new parents is completely normal.

She doesn't cite studies. She doesn't say "research indicates." She speaks the way someone speaks after decades of actually being there — plain, warm, a little dry sometimes, never performed.

And the rare thing that really does need a doctor's attention? She'll tell you clearly, once, without turning your evening into a crisis.

You typed: "she's been fighting sleep for a week, is something wrong?"
Great question! Sleep regressions are very common at this developmental stage. Research indicates that approximately 60% of infants experience disruptions due to neurological development. Here are some evidence-based strategies you can implement: 1) Establish a consistent bedtime routine, 2) Consider adjusting nap timing, 3) Review our sleep guide for age-appropriate wake windows.
Most babies go through a stretch around now where they fight sleep like it's the enemy. It doesn't mean you're doing something wrong. It means her brain is working faster than her body can handle, and the only way she knows to say so is to cry about it.

It gets better. Usually faster than it feels like it will.

What she actually does

A few things Patricia does every day

Not a feature list. A handful of things that make the early years feel a little less like you're doing it alone.

A daily note, in her voice
Every day, a short note from Patricia — written for your child's exact age, and read aloud in her voice if you'd rather listen than read. Less scrolling, more company.
Milestones as celebration
First smile, first steps, first word — marked as moments worth remembering, never as a scoreboard you can fall behind on.
Vaccines without the alarm
The standard schedule, plainly shown. No red banners, no "overdue" language, no shame. Just what's next, and what's already done.
Growth, as a place on a map
Percentiles shown the way your pediatrician actually reads them — a position, not a grade. Half of all healthy children sit below the 50th.
A record you'll want later
Record your own voice, and your child's. On graduation day, Patricia gives every one of them back to you — the newborn weeks, the first birthday, all of it.
Ready before the visit
A calm, one-page prep before every doctor's appointment — your questions on page one, the record behind them, nothing you'll forget in the parking lot.
How we're different, plainly stated

We will never tell you what a number means.

Nianza will help you record a temperature, a weight, a symptom. It will never tell you what that number means for your child. That's not caution dressed up as a feature — it's the whole point.

"That's not mine to decide. That's your doctor's job. I'm here for everything in between."

No diagnosis, ever No dosing advice, ever No outbreak alerts, ever No scores. No streaks.
The part most apps won't tell you

"This season has an end. Somewhere around five, my work here is finished — and I'd rather tell you that now than pretend otherwise."

Nianza isn't built to keep you forever. It's built to be exactly what these first years need, and then to say goodbye well — handing back everything you recorded along the way.
